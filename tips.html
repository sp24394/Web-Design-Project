General Gardening Tips


Watering & Plant Care

Students take turns tending to the garden's flowers and plants, ensuring everything stays healthy through regular watering and general upkeep.

Weeding & Maintenance

Keeping the garden tidy is an ongoing effort. Students work to clear weeds and maintain the beds so plants have the space and nutrients they need.

Planting & Landscaping

From placing seedlings to shaping new beds, students are hands-on in expanding and improving the garden's layout and variety of plants.

The Westlake Community Garden has grown into one of the school's most valued spaces, a place where students can step away from the classroom and contribute something tangible to the school community. Maintained entirely by students, the garden reflects a shared pride in the school grounds and a genuine care for the environment. Over the years it has expanded in both size and variety, with new plants, flowers and garden beds added each season by students who are passionate about seeing it thrive. It has become a living project that evolves with the students who tend to it, and that ongoing growth is something the whole school community can take pride in.

Beyond the physical work, the garden offers students a chance to develop responsibility, teamwork, and patience. There's a real satisfaction in watching something you've planted and cared for flourish over time, and that sense of achievement is something you can't get from a textbook. Students learn to pay attention to the needs of living things, to work carefully and consistently, and to take ownership of a shared space. These are skills that extend well beyond the garden itself and carry value into every area of life.

The garden is also a social space where students from different year levels work side by side toward a common goal. It has become a place where friendships are built as much as flower beds, and where the usual boundaries of school life feel a little less rigid. There's a relaxed, collaborative atmosphere that makes it welcoming to anyone who wants to get involved, whether they're a familiar face or joining for the very first time. Many students find it to be one of the more grounding and enjoyable parts of their week.

The garden is open to all students regardless of experience, and no background in gardening is needed to get started. New members are always welcome, and there's always something useful to do no matter your skill level. Whether you join for a single session out of curiosity or become a regular who shows up every week, your contribution genuinely makes a difference to the garden and to the people around you. If you're interested in getting involved, feel free to reach out through the contact form below or speak to a staff member directly.
